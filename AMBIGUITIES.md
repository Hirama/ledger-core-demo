# Ambiguities and the implemented policies

The request leaves several monetary, timing, and lifecycle policies unspecified. This implementation makes the following choices explicitly. They are demonstration policies, not statements of banking practice. In particular, append-only storage by itself does **not** decide whether fees should be refunded after a reversal.

## Time and historical knowledge

1. **Input order versus booking date.** E10 appears after E9 even though its supplied booking date is earlier. The replay preserves `E1` through `E10` exactly; it never sorts the input. Each record carries the supplied `booked_day`, the monetary `value_day`, and a monotonic `observed_day`. E10 is booked Day 5 and observed Day 6. Sorting it into Day 5 would change the knowledge available at the original Day 5 close.

2. **When a day closes.** Before accepting an event whose booking day advances the observed clock, the core closes all intervening days. `Finish()` closes the remaining days through Day 6 and then capitalizes interest. Thus the original Day 5 close occurs before E9, and before E10 is received. The ledger is a serialized command processor; it has no wall-clock scheduler.

3. **Meaning of a historical balance.** A value-date query includes monetary entries with value dates no later than the queried day. An optional sequence cutoff limits knowledge to records received by that point. Without that cutoff it uses everything currently known. The report shows both the original close and the final restated close; neither view overwrites the other. This distinction is essential when late postings change a previously closed day.

4. **What “before any fee” means in criterion 1.** E7 immediately triggers historical fee evaluation. There is therefore no separate public post-E7 state with all generated fees absent. The requested AED -370.00 is the principal-only Day 2 calculation, evaluated using the known source postings before generated fee entries: `1,200.00 - 950.00 - 620.00`. Tests check this counterfactual explicitly, separately from the actual post-assessment balance of AED -395.00.

5. **Historical fee reassessment.** A newly received cash posting can change multiple closed days. The core rechecks every closed day in ascending date order, for every account. It assesses a missing fee on each negative close and carries that fee into later balances. Fee eligibility is not restricted to the late posting's value date. This is the chosen reading of “once per day” and explains the rejection of criterion 2. A freeze-at-original-close policy would be a different model and would need explicit authorization.

6. **Day being assessed versus day assessment is observed.** A fee assessed for a restated Day 2 close receives value date Day 2 even though the assessment is observed Day 5. The report exposes both dates. “On Day 2” is ambiguous without this distinction: the fee belongs economically to Day 2 but was not known at the original Day 2 close.

7. **Fee feedback and order of closing work.** For each day the core first tests the current ledger balance, books any required fee, then computes interest on the balance including that fee. Existing earlier fees are included when later days are tested. A fee cannot trigger a second fee for the same account and day; an existing fee is the assessment marker. Negative balances earn zero interest.

8. **Reversal and past fees.** E9 appends the opposite principal posting with E7's value date. It does not remove E7, reverse fee entries, restore authorization decisions, or automatically refund charges. Fees already assessed remain valid under the selected policy even if restated principal subsequently becomes positive. A refund can be represented by another append-only credit; the request supplies neither a refund command nor eligibility policy, so the core does not invent one. This is a policy choice behind the rejection of criterion 6, not a consequence of append-only storage alone.

9. **What finality means.** Before finalization, historical interest can be corrected as later information arrives. After a successful `Finish()`, any new posting is recorded as a receipt plus `WINDOW_FINALIZED` error and produces no monetary entry. Repeated successful `Finish()` calls add nothing. `Finish()` reports a derived monetary overflow instead of wrapping an amount and leaves the ledger unfinalized on failure. The model has no reopen command, post-window adjustment period, or multi-period close.

10. **Allowed dates and malformed commands.** Event days must be integer day numbers within this six-day window, and `value_day <= booked_day`. Future value dates are rejected. Authorization lifecycle events also require equal value and booking dates. Before advancing the clock, the core checks that both dates are integers and satisfy the general window/order rule; date-invalid commands append only their receipt and error at the current observation day. A validly dated command can still advance the clock before a later business rejection, treating its supplied booking date as knowledge that time has advanced. Production intake would need a stricter clock authority.

## Money and interest

11. **Precision, grammar, and rounding mode.** AED is stored as integer fils at two decimal places and BHD as integer thousandths at three. The Go core stores money as `int64` minor units and parses decimal strings with exact standard-library `math/big` intermediates; command amounts have no binary-float representation. The grammar is `^[+-]?[0-9]+(?:\.[0-9]+)?$`: optional sign, digits, and an optional decimal point followed by digits. Fractions, exponents, whitespace, nonfinite strings, and bare decimal points are rejected. Excess input precision rounds to nearest with ties to even. The request specifies precision but not midpoint behavior; ties to even is the selected deterministic rule for both parsing and daily interest. A positive command amount that rounds to zero is rejected.

12. **The word “equal” in E10 and Go's zero-value count.** Three mathematically equal pieces of BHD 10.000 cannot all be represented at three decimals. Equal-as-possible means the pieces differ by at most one minor unit and preserve the total. Integer division gives `(3.334, 3.333, 3.333)`; the earliest piece receives the extra thousandth. All three are posted under E10, with the same dates, as separate immutable instalment records. No future instalment schedule is inferred. The generic splitter permits zero-value pieces when there are more pieces than available minor units; the source credit must still round to a positive total. On an `Event`, an instalment count of zero means the Go field was omitted and is normalized to one; explicit zero and absence are indistinguishable. Negative counts are rejected. The standalone splitting helper requires a strictly positive count.

13. **Accrued interest versus ledger money.** Daily interest is a memo accrual, not spendable ledger cash. Each day's target is the rounded positive closing ledger balance times the exact rate. Original accrual records remain; corrections append signed `INTEREST_ADJUSTMENT` records. For a day, its current rounded accrual is the sum of its original accrual and corrections. Authorizations use ledger cash and active holds, so they cannot spend uncapitalized interest.

14. **Which interest history to capitalize.** Capitalization uses the final restated daily accruals known at the end of the replay, not the originally reported accruals. E7 and E9 change historical interest; E10 contributes interest for both Day 5 and Day 6. The target excludes `INTEREST_CAPITALIZATION` entries, avoiding interest on that day's newly capitalized interest. There is no daily compounding before Day 6.

15. **Interest total versus remainder.** The capitalized amount is exactly the sum of rounded daily accruals, including their corrections. The code does not independently round an aggregate of unrounded accruals, so there is no residual to discard. Any such mismatch would violate the supplied conservation rule, not justify silently reducing the credit.

16. **The AED fee on a BHD account.** An AED 25.00 fee is specified, but no BHD fee or exchange rate is given. ACC-001 is configured with 2,500 AED minor units; ACC-002 has no configured overdraft fee. `FeeMinor == 0` represents missing fee configuration, not a zero-fee exemption. A negative BHD close appends `FEE_POLICY_ERROR` for that day and no cash debit. It does not silently charge BHD 25.000, convert AED, or assume exemption. The supplied BHD stream stays positive, so this missing policy is surfaced only by a synthetic test. A bank would need to resolve it before use.

17. **Sign and cash movement.** Credit, debit, authorization, and settlement amounts are positive magnitudes; record kind determines a cash posting's sign. Ordinary debits are permitted to overdraw because only authorization approval has an explicit available-balance constraint. Opening balances are account configuration, not journal events, and both supplied accounts start at zero. Accounts cannot exchange currency or transfer to one another in this model.

## Authorizations, reversals, and errors

18. **Hold validity after a late debit.** Authorization decisions use the ledger and all active holds at the observed time of the decision. E3 approves Auth-A with AED 50.00 available remaining. Later restatement may make the earlier account balance negative; it does not retroactively revoke that approval or settlement. This preserves the actual decision sequence and explains why E5 remains accepted.

19. **Settlement eligibility.** A settlement requires an existing active authorization on that account and an amount no greater than its hold. It does not run a second authorization-style available-balance approval. Auth-Z is unknown, so E6 records an error and makes no cash debit. Authorization identifiers are unique within an account, including declined and ended authorizations; different accounts can use the same identifier.

20. **A settlement smaller than its hold.** A single accepted settlement terminates the authorization and releases its entire hold. Auth-A's AED 185.00 settlement releases its AED 200.00 hold, and the remaining AED 15.00 is never debited. Partial captures followed by more captures, oversettlements, stand-in clearing, and tips are unsupported policies rather than inferred behavior. A rejected settlement leaves an active hold unchanged.

21. **Unsettled Auth-B.** With the actual preceding postings and assessments, E8 is declined because available cash is already negative. The attempted authorization and its failure remain in the journal; it has no active hold. In an independently funded scenario, an approved Auth-B would remain active indefinitely until an explicit settlement, cancellation, or expiry command. The window boundary alone does not release a hold.

22. **How a hold otherwise ends.** `CANCEL` represents an explicit cancellation and `EXPIRE` an explicit timeout decision from an external clock or operator. Both require an active authorization and append a terminal state while releasing the hold without moving cash. The core chooses no timeout duration and performs no automatic expiry. A declined authorization never enters the active state. No administrative override or abandonment transition is implemented.

23. **What a reversal may target.** The core permits one full reversal of a successfully posted credit or debit, including a credit split into instalments. It requires the same account, currency, and value date, computes the amount from the original postings, and rejects caller-supplied reversal amounts. It rejects duplicate reversals, unknown or failed targets, and reversals of settlements, fees, authorizations, or interest. Partial reversals and chargebacks require separate policies.

24. **Errors, retries, and audit scope.** Every post attempt appends its immutable `RECEIVED` event and, on failure, an `ERROR` record. A declined authorization additionally appends its decision record. IDs are reserved at first processing, even if validation fails; retrying an ID is an auditable duplicate error, not an idempotent replay of its prior result. The input order is authoritative. The Go core owns the journal internally and returns independent value snapshots. Events and records contain value fields rather than pointers or mutable maps shared with callers, so editing a returned snapshot cannot change the authoritative journal. Runtime caches and new journal appends may change, but prior records never do. This is an in-process API guarantee, not a tamper-proof audit log.

25. **Concurrency and scale.** The caller must serialize commands. There are no locks, parallel account writers, transactional persistence, checkpoints, deletion, or retention jobs. Balances and authorization states are reconstructed from the journal. These choices make the six-day audit straightforward but defer throughput, memory, and crash-recovery requirements to the architecture review.

26. **Finite monetary range.** Unlike the superseded Python prototype, the current Go core intentionally stores minor units in signed 64-bit integers. Parsing and intermediate arithmetic use exact `math/big` numbers, followed by checked conversion. Amounts outside `-2^63` through `2^63-1` are rejected. Cash commands preflight all affected daily balances before appending monetary entries, preventing a posting from silently overflowing a later balance. Available-balance subtraction is also checked; an overflow cannot create an approved hold. This is a representation limit rather than a bank limit, and production intake would need smaller explicit account and resource limits.

27. **Derived failures are not transactional rollbacks.** `Post(Event) error` and `Finish() error` report business and arithmetic failures explicitly. A rejected source cash posting does not append that posting, but a failure during subsequent fee/interest/close work can occur after earlier valid records have already been appended. Those records remain; the core does not roll back or silently wrap them. Even a clock-advance failure appends the attempted command's receipt and source error at the current observed day and reserves its ID. Finalization checks all capitalization credits before appending any, avoiding a partial payout; daily close snapshots are likewise prepared for all accounts before any snapshot is appended. Earlier fee/accrual work can still remain after a failed close, and finalization remains incomplete. A production adapter must inspect the journal after a returned error rather than treating every error as proof of no state change.
